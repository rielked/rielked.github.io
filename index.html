PRJ-01

LoRa Leak Detection System for Remote Pipelines

Final Year Project · BEng (Hons) EEE · 2025 · Individual

  • ESP32
  • SX1276 LoRa
  • 923 MHz
  • C++
  • Sensor fusion
  • Field testing

A two-node wireless system that detects gas leaks and mechanical disturbances on remote pipelines without gateways or internet. The transmitter samples a methane sensor (MQ-4), a pressure and temperature sensor (DPS310, I²C) and a piezo vibration sensor, packs the readings into a compact payload and sends it at 923 MHz. The receiver decodes each packet, shows live values on an OLED and sounds an LED and buzzer alarm when thresholds are crossed.

500 moutdoor link tested
100%detection accuracy up to 300 m
0false alarms in 250 trials
75–1,083 mslatency, SF7 to SF12

What I did

  • Selected components and drew the transmitter and receiver schematics (GPIO, ADC and I²C interfacing).
  • Wrote the C++ firmware: sampling, averaging, payload encoding, LoRa TX/RX and threshold alerts.
  • Planned and ran a three-phase field test: a distance sweep, an SF7–SF12 sweep and a 250-trial confusion-matrix evaluation.
  • Computed accuracy, precision, recall and specificity, and analysed the results against LoRa link-budget theory.
PRJ-02

IoT Service Module: Autonomous Shape-Guided Robot

Capstone (Integrated System Design Project) · 2025 · Team of 3 · My subsystems: vision and navigation

  • Raspberry Pi 5
  • YOLO
  • Python
  • Viam cloud
  • Ultrasonic
  • L298N / PWM

A Raspberry Pi 5 robot that travels to a remote plant whose temperature sensor has failed, finding its way by recognising triangle, circle and square markers, then takes over the plant's cooling-fan control from a base-station laptop. I owned two of the four subsystems: the vision pipeline that detects and streams, and the navigation logic that turns detections into motor commands.

2 of 4subsystems owned
3 shapescustom YOLO classes
120°camera field of view
Livevideo to base station via Viam

What I did

  • Trained a custom YOLO model on triangle, circle and square markers and ran inference on the Pi.
  • Chose YOLO over OpenCV contour detection, and Viam over Blynk and Arduino Cloud, after comparing accuracy, video support and cost.
  • Wrote Python navigation logic fusing YOLO detections with ultrasonic ranging to centre on targets and stop, pivot or slow near obstacles.
  • Documented limits from testing (poor light, the sensor's 10 cm dead zone, reflective surfaces) and proposed fixes.
PRJ-03

Direct Air Capture Prototype: CO₂ Monitoring Electronics

Engineering Team Project · September 2023 · Team of 7 · Assistant Project Director, sole electrical engineer

  • Arduino Nano
  • C++
  • MQ135
  • OLED + LCD
  • BJT switching
  • Instrumentation

A tabletop chamber that pulls ambient air across a 5% potassium hydroxide solution to absorb CO₂. I designed and programmed the electronics: an Arduino Nano reads an MQ135 gas sensor, averages 10 samples, converts the result to ppm and shows it live on an OLED bar graph and a 16×2 LCD. Three LED levels and a 3 kHz buzzer warn at rising CO₂, and a transistor switch runs the fan and pump from a 7.4 V battery.

45%CO₂ reduction, closed test (840 → 462 ppm)
35%reduction, open air (611 → 396 ppm)
3 levelsalarm at 1,000 and 2,000 ppm
RM269total build cost

What I did

  • Drew the circuit and driver schematics and wrote the C++ firmware: sampling, averaging, ppm mapping, alarms and dual display.
  • Compared the MQ135, MG811 and MH-Z19B sensors and chose the MQ135 for cost (RM7.50) and a 20 s warm-up.
  • Calculated the fan and pump load, base current and forced beta for the switching stage.
  • As assistant project director, set weekly agendas, assigned tasks and tracked progress across four disciplines.

PRJ-04

12 V Linear DC Bench Power Supply

Electrical Circuit Analysis · March 2023 · Team of 4

  • Full-wave rectifier
  • L7812 / LM317T
  • Eagle PCB
  • Soldering
  • Oscilloscope
  • Power

A bench supply that steps 240 V mains down through a centre-tapped transformer, rectifies it with a full-wave rectifier, smooths it with a capacitor filter and regulates it into a fixed 12 V rail (L7812) and an adjustable rail (LM317T) that drives a cooling fan. Designed on paper, proved on a breadboard, laid out in Eagle, then soldered into a ventilated acrylic enclosure with panel meters.

12.68 Vfixed output measured (+5.7%)
1.25–13.75 Vadjustable range (design)
14–30 V RMSsafe input window
240 V ACmains input

What I did

  • Sized the LM317 resistors from the datasheet equation (R1 = 200 Ω, R2 = 2 kΩ for 13.75 V maximum).
  • Set the input window so the rectified peak (19.8–42.4 V) clears regulator dropout and stays below the 50 V capacitor rating.
  • Traced a no-output fault to swapped regulators and repaired lifted copper traces.
  • Rebuilt the enclosure around measured component dimensions after the first case failed to fit.

Design calculations

LM317 output
VO = 1.25 (1 + R2/R1) with R2 = 2 kΩ, R1 = 200 Ω gives 13.75 V maximum
Minimum input
14 V × 1.414 = 19.8 V peak, enough headroom above regulator dropout
Maximum input
30 V × 1.414 = 42.4 V peak, below the 50 V filter-capacitor rating
PRJ-05

Wordle Game in C with an X11 Interface

Structured Programming · September–December 2022 · Team of 2

  • C
  • X11 graphics
  • File I/O
  • Event loop
  • Linux

A complete Wordle clone written in C on Linux. The player gets six tries to guess a hidden five-letter word using an on-screen keyboard; each guess is validated against a 200-word list and coloured tile by tile. Everything on screen is drawn with a small X11 graphics library, and all input comes from mouse clicks mapped to on-screen coordinates. Screenshots below are from a fresh build of the original code.

~680lines of C
200words loaded from file
4validation checks per guess
0repeated target words

What I did

  • Event loop that turns mouse clicks into actions by hit-testing button and key coordinates.
  • On-screen QWERTY keyboard with Enter and delete keys, writing letters into the tile grid as they are typed.
  • Random word selection from the file, tracking used words so none repeats until all 200 are played.
  • Letter-by-letter comparison producing green, yellow and grey feedback for each tile.
